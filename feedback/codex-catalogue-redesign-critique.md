## Verdict

The catalogue direction is viable, but the redesign currently presents an eclectic artist with personal brands more convincingly than a senior creative leader who brings products to market. Your strongest evidence—revision drawings, stylization decisions and shipped objects—sits beneath oversized introductions, uneven curation and claims the pages do not adequately substantiate. The most urgent correction is factual: Doorables was a 2019 TOTY Collectible of the Year nominee; the official winner was LEGO’s Harry Potter and Fantastic Beasts Minifigure Series.

Sources:
- [Toy Association official 2019 winners release](https://toyfair.vporoom.com/ToyFair/L-O-L-Surprise-by-MGA-Entertainment-Wins-Prestigious-Toy-of-the-Year-Award)
- [Moose Toys nomination announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/moose-toys-dominates-with-five-toty-nominations-300748878.html)

**Overall: 6/10.** The visual foundation is stronger than the editorial argument, attribution and supporting evidence.

## Findings across all 14 lenses

### 1. First impressions

In five seconds, I understand: “Artist who works for Disney and has two personal projects.” I do not understand your level of responsibility. The large name and portrait establish the person before the professional proposition. — Home · desktop · part 1.

In 30 seconds, TAD appears to be the main practice, VNCE the second, and Disney the third. The same order becomes more consequential on phone because readers encounter it sequentially. — Home · phone · parts 1–2.

An hour later, I would probably remember the hooded TAD figure and skull astronaut before remembering a specific product decision you made. The one-word impression is **eclectic**. That is weaker than inventive, authoritative or distinctive.

### 2. Positioning and narrative

Artist is demonstrated. Designer is demonstrated once readers reach the development drawings. World builder is asserted more strongly than demonstrated.

The missing story is how your judgment travels between disciplines: what you recognize in a character, how you establish rules for a product family, and how those rules survive manufacturing. Your Incredibles overlays and Frozen redlines contain that story already. They deserve more prominence than another paragraph announcing three practices. — Disney & Beyond · desktop · parts 3–6.

Keep all three bodies of work, but give them different jobs: Disney proves professional judgment; TAD proves independent authorship and production; VNCE proves original character thinking.

### 3. Taste filter

Barlow Condensed and Newsreader work together. The condensed face supplies force; the serif supplies intimacy and reading rhythm. Keep them.

The weakness is the familiarity of the total package: oversized condensed capitals, dark ground, italic serif captions, numbered plates and fine rules resemble the established Cargo/editorial portfolio vocabulary. This is a visual association, not evidence of a particular template. The museum styling becomes distinctive only when the annotations and sequencing express your judgment.

TAD’s classical busts, blackletter, distressed graphics, butterflies and memento mori language sit near familiar Gallery Dept./Represent streetwear territory. The X-eyed bubble graphic adds a KAWS-adjacent association. This is not an allegation of copying; it identifies the competitive visual neighborhood. Show fewer graphics and explain the specific idea behind the strongest ones. — TAD · desktop · parts 3–4.

The paper rotation is less persuasive than the real marks on the paper. Repeating slight tilts across unrelated drawings can make handmade feel like a preset.

### 4. Identity and system

The dark base and restrained accent are consistent. Resolve these inconsistencies:

| Inconsistency | Evidence and correction |
|---|---|
| Disney and Pixar versus Disney & Beyond | Work · phone · part 1 versus Disney page title. Use one navigational category name. |
| Brand pages and individual images share one index | Work · desktop · parts 1–2. TAD is a whole practice; VNCE, floating is one picture. Choose a consistent browsing unit. |
| Plate numbering disappears in some contexts | TAD drops · desktop · parts 1–2; Press · phone · part 1. Define archive and press exceptions deliberately. |
| Mounts do not always communicate medium | Finished VNCE character views receive paper mounts; the Snow White pencil/final composite receives one paper treatment. VNCE · desktop · parts 1–2; Disney · desktop · part 5. |
| Work loses its active navigation state on project pages | Source comparison fails on ../portfolio.html. Normalize destination URLs. |
| Press’s displayed image and description diverge | Press · desktop · part 1, browser interaction. Fix the responsive image update. |
| Thumbnail and destination disagree | Noir links to the Disney illustration section, which does not contain that image. Work · desktop · part 2. |
| Shopping language disagrees with availability | Shop TAD leads to signup and sold-out products. TAD · desktop · part 1; TAD drops · desktop · part 1. |

Plate numbers reset on each page. That is reasonable, but they currently provide atmosphere more than retrieval. Descriptive captions should do the substantive work.

The Snow White Work thumbnail crops away the head, reducing the work to a dress crop. Show the complete composition or a deliberate detail identified as such. — Work · desktop · part 1; phone · part 2.

### 5. Generated or template tells

I cannot determine how an image was made from its appearance alone. The problem here is authorship ambiguity and generic presentation:

- The oversized hooded TAD image resembles fashion concept imagery more than evidence of a particular apparel practice.
- The Character, The World and The Work organize VNCE without revealing anything specific about him.
- Repeated descriptions of three practices, partner names and tenure feel assembled from interchangeable biography blocks.
- Uniformly tilted paper and repeated display/body compositions make the system more visible than the editorial decisions.

The solution is specificity: actual decisions, artifacts, dates, limitations and consequences.

### 7. Copy and voice

Your best existing line is “I draw it, sculpt it and see it through to the shelf.” It describes a tangible capability. Build the presentation around proving those verbs.

The weakest habits are repeated introductions, vague future-facing language, and stock personal phrasing such as partner-in-crime. “I carried it solo” needs narrower scope so readers understand which responsibility was yours. — About · desktop · part 1; Disney & Beyond · desktop · part 3.

Three replacements:

| Current line | Replacement |
|---|---|
| Apparel, art prints and whatever I’m ready to put into the world. | TAD is my independent label for apparel and art prints. |
| Things you can feel before you can explain them. | The first collection pairs classical sculpture with graffiti across apparel and prints. |
| A character built to carry his own world. | I created VNCE in 2023 and am developing him through illustration, model sheets and merchandise. |

The replacements sacrifice atmosphere to establish facts. Let the images supply atmosphere. Verify any replacement against existing project context before publishing.

### 8. Information architecture

Work should lead visitors into a small number of substantial cases. Currently it alternates between brands, projects and individual pictures. Two VNCE images take separate index positions while multiple professional projects disappear inside one enormous Disney page. — Work · desktop · parts 1–2.

Split the strongest professional projects into addressable cases, even if the catalogue remains the visual framework. Add a résumé/CV link on About, with a public version that respects your title constraint.

Press does not currently earn a main-navigation position. Two older, unlinked summaries could become a compact Selected recognition section on About. TAD drops is a secondary audience destination and should receive navigation weight accordingly.

### 9. Phone experience

The phone layouts mostly reflow competently. The problem is editorial burden:

- Home makes visitors pass the portrait, TAD and VNCE before professional product evidence. — Home · phone · parts 1–2.
- Work’s filters wrap Illustration onto a separated second line. Use a compact wrapping group with consistent row spacing. — Work · phone · part 1.
- TAD takes eight parts; Disney takes ten. Much of that depth is optional archive material. — TAD · phone · parts 1–8; Disney & Beyond · phone · parts 1–10.
- Disney’s introduction, metadata and nine-item index delay the first case. Put a compelling product/process pair before the extended context. — Disney & Beyond · phone · parts 1–3.
- Contact’s separate social links and portrait push the form substantially downward. Put the form immediately after the contact introduction; place the portrait afterward. — Contact · phone · parts 1–2.

The browser measured the hamburger at 34 × 25 CSS pixels. Increase its practical hit area to 44–48 pixels.

### 10. Accessibility

The foundations are better than the quiet styling might suggest. The inspected source includes skip links, labeled inputs, autocomplete, descriptive alt text, visible focus styling and reduced-motion handling. The mobile menu supports Escape and focus return. The portrait supports keyboard activation and becomes static under reduced motion.

Calculated contrast against the near-black background:

| Element | Ratio |
|---|---:|
| Primary text | 16.40:1 |
| Secondary text | 8.02:1 |
| Muted text | 5.74:1 |
| Teal accent | 5.05:1 |

These exceed the normal-text AA threshold. Control borders against the form surface measure approximately 3.37:1. Teal on warm paper is approximately 3.25:1, so keep small teal text on the dark ground. See [WCAG 2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/).

The confirmed accessibility defect is Press’s image switch: its alt text changes to Snow White while the visible responsive image remains Doorables. Verify 200% text enlargement and 320-pixel reflow before declaring overall conformance. The hamburger recommendation is a usability improvement; 44-pixel targets are not a blanket WCAG 2.1 AA requirement.

### 11. Credibility, legal and IP presentation

The award correction is mandatory. A nominee designation remains worthwhile; an incorrect winner claim makes the rest of the portfolio less trustworthy.

Other attribution questions:

- Clients mixes employers and commercial partners. Use Studios and partners unless those were direct client relationships. — Disney & Beyond · desktop · part 1.
- $500 million needs scope. Define retail versus another sales measure, period, covered products and your relationship to them. The supplied materials do not independently substantiate it. — Home · desktop · part 2; About · desktop · part 1.
- The full Snow White set includes other artists. Keep your contribution unmistakable when showing the packaging and 22-piece set. — Disney & Beyond · desktop · part 5.
- Revision sheets, tech packs and concept work need publication clearance checked against actual permissions. Their presence does not establish that they are confidential, or that they are cleared.
- Animation-ready, sculpture and screen claims exceed the visible VNCE evidence. Show the corresponding artifacts or narrow the wording. — VNCE · desktop · parts 1–2.

No forbidden exact job title or disallowed AI copy was found in the inspected redesign markup. The clicked Contact portrait is treated as the exception expressly described in the brief. Do not remove it merely because dormant source comments mention self-drawing.

### 12. Conversion

Direct email is effective. Tell me what you’re making is a useful invitation. Keep both.

Shop TAD is inaccurate while the destination contains a waitlist and sold-out archive. Rename it Get drop updates or View Season 00. The signup needs a specific proposition: what subscribers will hear about, without inventing a launch date.

Contact and waitlist forms depend on Netlify processing. Their markup does not prove delivery on the intended host. Verify receipt and success/error behavior before launch; no submissions were made during review. — Contact · desktop · part 1; TAD drops · desktop · part 1.

### 13. Blind spots

Recognizable IP is doing too much persuasive work. A hiring manager already knows Disney can make compelling products. They need to understand what became better because you were involved.

The unanswered questions are consequential: What did you originate? What did you inherit? What decisions could you approve? How did you direct partners? Which artifacts are yours? What kind of work do you want next?

The long 2012–2014 exhibition list creates an unintended recency problem: it makes the achievement timeline feel older than your active career. — About · desktop · part 2; phone · parts 3–4.

### 14. Benchmark

These are specific comparisons, not a claim to rank the entire field:

| Reference | What it demonstrates more effectively |
|---|---|
| [Gary Ham / Superham](https://www.superham.com/) | Distinguishes production toys, handmade objects, collaborations and character properties. Your categories blur those distinctions. |
| [Joe Ledbetter](https://www.joeledbetter.com/about/) | Connects a recognizable visual language with named releases, collaborators and licensing opportunities. TAD needs comparable specificity. |
| [Matt Jones / Lunartik](https://lunartik.com/about/) | Explains the path from character idea through self-production and manufacturing. Your development artifacts could tell an equally useful story. |
| [Jason Freeny](https://www.jasonfreeny.com/sculpture) | Identifies individual works with dates and materials, making the physical practice explicit. Your sculpture claims need equivalent evidence. |

The redesign’s advantage is its coherent catalogue presentation and the potential of its professional revision material. Its shortfall is that visitors must infer too much about authorship, production and responsibility.

Compared with the [current live site](https://zachgracia.com/), the redesign simplifies the presentation but loses useful professional category distinctions. Recover that clarity within the catalogue direction.

## Technical evidence and implementation pointers

These are concrete findings, not additional speculative design objections.

### Press entries are not source links

`publications.html` around lines 66 and 78 uses focusable `div.pub-card` elements. Browser inspection found no href. Replace or supplement them with actual coverage links including publication, title, date and author when known.

### Press responsive-image mismatch

`publications.html` around line 135 updates `img.src`, alt text and caption but leaves the original `srcset`. After focusing/selecting Snow White, the browser reported:
- src: Snow White asset.
- alt/caption: Snow White.
- currentSrc and visible image: Frozen Doorables drawover.

Update/remove srcset alongside src. Ensure the image mount remains appropriate for the selected medium.

### Project navigation state

`js/main.js` around line 76 checks `href === 'portfolio.html'` on project pages, whose navigation uses `../portfolio.html`. Normalize URLs before comparing. Preserve the distinction between current-page and parent-section semantics.

### Phone target

Hamburger measured 34 × 25 CSS px at 390 × 844. Enlarge the interactive area to 44–48 px without requiring a larger visible icon.

### Forms

`contact.html` around line 72 and `cart.html` around line 54 use POST and Netlify form attributes. Source alone does not establish deployment processing. Test on intended hosting before stating delivery works.

### Accessibility strengths to preserve

- Skip links, document language, labeled inputs and autocomplete.
- Visible global focus outline.
- Mobile menu moves focus, makes main/footer inert, supports Escape and focus return.
- Contact sheet supports button semantics and Enter/Space.
- Contact sheet respects reduced motion and uses a static image.
- Current Home portrait is static; dormant hero-drawing comments are not proof of active self-drawing motion.
- No missing local HTML image src assets were found.
